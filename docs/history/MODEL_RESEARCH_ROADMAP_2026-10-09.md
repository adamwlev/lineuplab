#### Bias follow-up — exploratory evidence, 2026-10-06

Adam asked to investigate the early v20 negative bias more deeply. New read-only
saved-forecast diagnostics use retained J_POINT_BOTH **single-checkpoint,
three-seed physical ledgers**, not a new v20 arm or a changed scorer. Across
44 quarters / 2,505,033 FGA-and-location-observed possessions, conditional FG
conversion error is +0.023 points/100 overall, but −1.029 in the final >=50
lineup-quarter cohort and +0.590 below it. The gap is negative in 41/44 quarters
and 11/11 seasons; descriptive season-bootstrap interval [−1.909, −1.365].
This is a shooting-conversion component, not total-rating bias or a causal effect.

Matching player and shot-location within quarters leaves a −0.682 percentage-point
conversion-residual gap. Selection based on **prior-quarter** >=50 exposure gives
a smaller −0.519 versus +0.101 points/100 gap (negative in 10/11 seasons); before-
game current-quarter exposure similarly reduces the gap. Populations differ,
so the reduction is not an identified estimate of selection bias. First-three-
game residuals barely predict later residuals (harmonic-exposure correlation .012).
These findings motivate separating deployment/selection and persistent context
from individual hot shooting; they do not support a bonus for future exposure,
known-pair chemistry, changing the frozen cohort, or a generic recent-bias fix.

The motivating 2017q2 shortfall persists in later games and is distributed across
players; the five largest player contributions explain only 19.3% of its net FG
shortfall. Roughly half comes from three-point shooting. It is more severe than
the average cohort gap. All results are post-hoc development evidence; original
ledgers, calibration, full-run cohorts and all three acceptance standards remain.
