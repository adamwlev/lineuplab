# Cross-era check — 2026-10-12

Exploratory but decision-relevant (docs/CLOSEOUT.md, step 3). The input rule
and the decision rule were declared in the script before scoring.

- Script: `scripts/cross_era.py`.
- Output: `experiments/cache/cross_era_2026_10_12/` (`result.json`,
  `report.txt`, per-row pickles).

## Question

The tool puts player-seasons from any era into one model. What does that cost
on real lineups with outcomes? And should a player's state be used as it was,
or translated to the model's league?

## Design

**Direction A, the tool's direction.**
- Model: the release model (R at 2027q1, trained 2021–2026).
- Targets: 2007–2013 lineups (28 quarters, 5,476 broad cells).
- Inputs: era-store origin states.
- Baseline: the era backtest's in-era R forecasts on the same rows.

**Direction B.**
- Model: the era fits at 2013q4 (trained 2008–2013).
- Targets: 2016–2026 lineups (44 quarters, 8,345 broad cells).
- Inputs: v16 origin states.
- Baseline: the saved v16 reference.

**Forecasts.** The calibrated network alone, mean of three seeds, against the
defenses actually faced. Capability banks are read at each player's own
origin. The environment and calibration are the model's.

**Rules.**
- *As-was:* raw states through the model's normalizer.
- *Translated:* each raw coordinate shifted by the model's mean minus the
  player's origin mean (normalizer means). The ledger's raw anchors are
  shifted too.

**Checks.**
- An era fit run through this path at its own origin reproduces its saved
  forecast to 1e-8.
- At the model's own origin, translated equals as-was exactly.

**Decision rule.** Adopt translated only if it beats as-was on centered r² in
both directions.

## Results

Broad offense-five cells (at least 50 possessions), points per 100. Centered
r² is within quarter. Differences are pooled, with season mean ± season SE.

| | A: release on 2007–2013 | B: 2013 era fits on 2016–2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| In-era baseline, centered r² | .1357 | .1012 |
| As-was | .0903 | .0567 |
| Translated | .1012 | .0464 |
| As-was − baseline | −.045 ± .004 (0/7) | −.045 ± .006 (0/11) |
| Translated − as-was | +.011 ± .005 (6/7) | −.010 ± .005 (3/11) |
| Within-quarter correlation: baseline / as-was / translated | .369 / .311 / .324 | .318 / .274 / .273 |
| Excess cell MSE over baseline, as-was ((points per 100)²) | +5.3 | +5.9 |

- **Decision: as-was.** Translation helps in A and hurts in B. In B, the
  correlation is unchanged and the loss is a scale effect. The tool uses
  players' states as they were, in the release model's league.
- **Cross-era cost.**
  - A model forecasting another era keeps about 56–66% of the in-era centered
    r² and 84–86% of the within-quarter correlation.
  - The loss does not grow with the gap beyond about three years. In A,
    2007, the farthest season, loses least. In B, the losses are noisy:
    −0.014 to −0.069 by season.
- **Band term.** Cross-era players add about 5–6 (points per 100)² of model
  error per side per cell. The in-support persistent error is about 10
  (`scripts/band_in_support.py`), so the per-side SD rises from about 3.2 to
  about 3.9.
- **Levels across eras are not validated.** Centering removes each quarter's
  level. What a 2008 lineup scores in the 2026 league has no outcome to check
  against. The tool must say so.

## A problem this exposed

Every era lineup in A and every recent lineup in B is "beyond every trained
lineup" in composition distance (`lineups.support`). The era shift dominates
the distance, so the "unusual composition" reason would fire for every
historical lineup.

For the tool, the composition reason will be measured against each player's
own season, and the era gap will be reported as its own "cross-era" reason.
